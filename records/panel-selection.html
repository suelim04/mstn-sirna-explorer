Marginal-coverage panel selection

Eight sequence-eligible windows were selected using weighted marginal feature-bin coverage, followed by two redundancy tie-breaks and deterministic lexical handling. This is a transparent diversity criterion, not a D-optimal or mutual-information design. The reporter-evaluation outcome was already known before this selection; the method was not blind to that result.

Four of the eight members fall in a single merged cluster, spanning [328,359), or 31 nucleotides. Each consecutive pair overlaps by fifteen bases, below the sixteen-base redundancy threshold. All six pairs within this cluster fall below that threshold. The panel therefore samples five merged locations, not eight independent sites.

The result covers all 39 active bins and all 180 units of coverage weight, with zero pairs under both declared redundancy definitions. Those upper/lower bounds provide an objective-relative optimality certificate at fixed n = 8. The optimum need not be unique. The lexical rule was applied deterministically but not proven minimal.

Marginal bin coverage does not penalize sequence similarity below the overlap threshold. Closely related windows can occupy different GC, seed or conservation bins and count as diverse. This is a limitation of the criterion, not an arithmetic error in the result. Pool-derived bin boundaries were fixed before selection; they were not externally derived. That fact does not remove the known-outcome limitation.

Divergence from a low-count comparator is expected from contrasting objectives: coverage rewards representation of nonzero-match bins while the comparator prioritises lower counts. Neither objective estimates potency. Eight measurements across correlated features cannot attribute effects to individual axes, and an MSTN-only readout cannot validate off-target-match dimensions.

The later selection of [455,474) is a separate construction preference; it did not replace this panel or establish a superior biological candidate.
