4. Strand asymmetry: descriptive, not an anomaly class

No predeclared biological expectation or null requiring similar guide/passenger counts was found in the frozen plan, the original pre-result inspection conventions, or the committed extraction implementation. The declared (count+1)/(comparison+1) ratio trigger (at least 10 or at most 0.1, with the larger raw count at least 10) is a numerical selection convention; it is not evidence that the two strands should have similar counts. The strands query different target patterns.

The 952 historical strand-asymmetry rows are reclassified as DESCRIPTIVE strand differences in the reporting overlay. The current anomaly-review-trigger total is therefore 508 local-neighborhood rows, not 1,460 anomalies. Local triggers are review heuristics, not statistically calibrated anomaly probabilities. Original ratios, source rows, selections, 41 inspected cases and eligibility remain unchanged. Coverage is restated in section 2: local 20/508 rows and 20 cases; descriptive 12/952 rows and 24 cases, with three cases shared.

8. Method assigning orthologous sites

Gene orthology was established separately by the saved official NCBI Gene orthology table (human MSTN 2660 → mouse 17700). Site assignment then used the custom global pairwise nucleotide dynamic program in src/alignment.py (Needleman–Wunsch-type recurrence) between human ENST00000260950.5 and mouse NM_010834.3.

The corresponding annotated 5′UTR, complete CDS including stop, and 3′UTR were aligned separately. Scores were +2 canonical match, −1 substitution/noncanonical pair, −3 per gap base, including terminal gaps; gaps are linear, not affine. Forward/prefix and backward/suffix optimum tables retain all equally optimal coordinate/gap assignments per human base. There is no arbitrary tie-breaking traceback.

Multiple possible assignments or noncanonical site bases yield INCOMPLETE. A unique gap or nonconsecutive comparator coordinates fails the ungapped conservation rule. Only a unique contiguous 19-base comparator interval receives a mismatch count. Exact mouse identity is the existing eligibility gate; relaxed and other-species columns stay informational. This operational assignment depends on the chosen references, annotations and declared scores; it is not experimental proof of unique evolutionary alignment. Existing implementation and stored alignments were inspected without rerunning them.

9. Conserved 3′UTR observation and assay carry-forward

Passing starts 2232–2235 span one human 22-nt block [2232,2254), matching mouse [2171,2193) exactly. They are four overlapping windows, not four independent conserved elements. Conservation makes regulatory function a concern to carry forward, but does not establish the specific function of this block.

Targeting this block could perturb endogenous regulation independently of RNA depletion. A control distinguishing those explanations remains to be designed; no regulatory function was established here.

Search and interpretation boundaries

  • “100,285 19-base search positions in the target transcriptome containing noncanonical bases were excluded.” All 2,801 query windows were searched on both strands.
  • No published reagent was declared eligible under the chosen audit architecture. Ineligibility reflects the architecture, mechanism and conservation requirement, not reagent quality; unresolved source definitions do not disprove published activity. Five records remain incomplete rather than established failures, and two are mechanism exclusions.
  • Engine correctness within the declared contract is established by the constructed, differential and deliberately broken-engine test suites, intended-interval recovery and output verification. Flag inspection does not establish engine correctness. Its coverage limits interpretation of patterns, not the validity of those engine checks.
